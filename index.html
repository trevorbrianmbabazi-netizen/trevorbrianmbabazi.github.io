01 · CREDIT RISK
Where a consumer loan book went wrong, and when

13,432 loans, UGX 24bn originated across four channels. The board saw an 8.9% default rate and a growing book. Both numbers were hiding the same problem.

The question
Portfolio default was rising but originations were growing, so ratios looked stable on a trailing basis. Is this a collections problem or an underwriting problem?
Approach
Vintage analysis, grouping loans by origination cohort and tracking cumulative default against months on book so each cohort is compared at the same age rather than the same calendar date. Then channel decomposition, early payment default isolation, and a risk versus price check by score band.
Underwriting, not collections. The July to December 2025 cohorts default 5 to 8 points higher than every prior cohort at the same age. The damage was locked in at origination during a growth push that dropped mean credit score by 42 points.
One channel carries it. The agent network writes at 58 points lower score and defaults at 13.9% against roughly 6 to 7% elsewhere, on a third of the book. Nearly half its defaults occur within three months, which points at origination quality or fraud rather than borrower affordability.
Subprime is underpriced. It carries 4.8 times the default rate of prime but only 1.7 times the price, so the band is written below its risk adjusted cost.

02 · INSURANCE
A motor book losing money for nine months before anyone noticed

5,283 claims across six product lines and four regions, 18 months of experience. Portfolio loss ratio 64%, comfortably profitable and completely misleading.

The question
Which product lines and regions are actually carrying the book, and which are being subsidised by the others?
Approach
Cleaned 5,325 raw claim records: de-duplication, product name standardisation, region imputation from branch, and large loss capping decisions documented rather than applied silently. Then loss ratio decomposition by product, region and month against earned premium, plus frequency and severity separation and scheme level experience rating.
Motor Comprehensive in Central climbed from 70% to 127% loss ratio over 18 months while pricing stayed flat. It was underwritten at the same rate for a year and a half while the claims moved underneath it.
Vehicle theft was the mechanism. It made up 14% of motor claims before September 2025 and 40% after, and theft claims settle far larger than collision.
One medical scheme runs at double the book average severity across 435 claims. That is a renewal pricing conversation with a number attached to it.

03 · PROGRAMME PERFORMANCE
What an 88% attainment figure was concealing

4,286 activity records across eight districts and four delivery partners, eight quarters of a multi year programme. The quarterly report said 88.5%. That was true and useless.

The question
Two thirds of the programme period has elapsed. Is delivery on track, and if not, where specifically is it failing?
Approach
Attainment measured per district against target rather than in aggregate, budget absorption benchmarked against time elapsed rather than against budget alone, and reporting lag measured per partner to expose data quality risk.
The average hid two failing districts. One delivered 41% of target and another 71%, while the remaining six sat above 92%. Those same two districts were the only ones below 40% female participation and carried the highest cost per beneficiary.
One output will close with half its money unspent, at 34% absorbed against 66.7% of the period elapsed.
One partner reports 38 days late against roughly a week for the other three, delaying every downstream verification cycle.
